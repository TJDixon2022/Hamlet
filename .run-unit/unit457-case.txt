3. THE RULE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One construction: the scored send is preceded, as on the air, by the same operator's earlier
keying at the same level and speed, ending one word gap (7 units) before the scored send's first
element, and filling the 5 s before it. The earlier keying is the same send once: the case keys
`PARIS CQ PARIS CQ`, and the scored text is the second `PARIS CQ`. Nothing else changes from
456's recipe: the same speed, pitch, level, edges, noise, seed, SNR, lead-in and tail.

Why the same send once: at 18 WPM `PARIS CQ` is 77 units, 5.13 s, so with the word gap it fills
the 5 s window the control measured, and it holds 40 units of mark, 2.67 s, inside the control's
1.30 to 3.46 s. It adds no text chosen for the port: repeating a call is the on-air habit the
control's 17:37 (CQ CQ CQ) and 012403 (DE KD0UN KD0UN) show.

The earlier send is not scored and not asserted: fldigi's reading of it is printed only. That is
the first element after seconds of noise, which task 2 has already shown fldigi loses, and the
real recordings never ask it to read.

The scored span, fixed here: the last ten characters fldigi prints, which must be ` PARIS CQ ` -
the word space fldigi prints for the 7-unit gap before the scored send (more than 4 dot lengths,
cw.cxx:910-914), the key, and the word space the 1.5 s tail gives. The leading space is what makes
the span the scored send's own: it proves P was not joined to anything before it. Expected text
of the scored send, spaced by fldigi's own rules: `PARIS CQ ` preceded by its word space.

4. THE RECIPE (enough to rebuild it)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Send: `PARIS CQ PARIS CQ`, patterns from CW_SPEC.md 6 (ITU-R M.1677-1), typed in the test,
  not from fldigi's morse.cxx and not from MorseAlphabet (CLAUDE.md 12.5):
  P .--.  A .-  R .-.  I ..  S ...  C -.-.  Q --.-
  Plain letters, one word space inside each send, one word space between the two sends.
  The rendered marks are measured back into letters and checked against the patterns before the
  port is run.
- Speed: 18 WPM, fldigi's shipped CWspeed (configuration.h:513). One unit 1.2/18 s = 66.67 ms.
  Marks 1 and 3 units; gaps 1 (element), 3 (character), 7 (word).
- Pitch: 600 Hz, given to the port as fldigi's waterfall cursor would give it.
- Level and edges: peak 0.5; raised-cosine edges of 5 ms at each end of every mark.
- Rate: 8000 Hz, fldigi's own, so no resampling.
- Lead-in: 10.0 s of noise before the first mark of the earlier send (456's).
- Tail: 1.5 s of noise after the last mark of the scored send (456's).
- Noise: white Gaussian from xorshift32 (state seeded with 20260926, shifts 13, 17, 5; uniform
  ((state & 0xFFFFFF) + 1) / 16777217) and Box-Muller in pairs; shaped to 300-2800 Hz by a
  257-tap Blackman-windowed sinc band-pass; scaled so tone power (peak^2 / 2) over band-noise power
  is +10 dB. In a 2500 Hz band that is +10 dB at the CW_SPEC.md 8.1 reference. Never digital
  silence (V-06). Seed 20260926, 456's, not searched.
- The port: FldigiCwDecoder(600), fldigi's shipped progdefaults at 61b97f41, squelch off per
  benchmark.cxx:54 (456 DECIDED (1)), all audio given in one rx_process call.

5. HOW IT IS RUN
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TheSecondDecoderIsAFaithfulPortTests.ItReadsASendWhoseKeyIsExact, naming HM-REQ-122, is changed to
this case. First with a deliberately wrong expectation, ` PARIS CQX`, watched red; then with
