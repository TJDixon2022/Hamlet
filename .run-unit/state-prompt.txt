## 2. What the owner should expect

### What happens when you press CQ under PSK31 tonight

You press the PSK31 chip. The panel tunes to 14.070 and starts listening across the
whole passband, as it has since unit 316.

You press **Call CQ**. In the moment between the press and the carrier:

- Hamlet **looks at the band and picks a spot to call on**. It takes every station
  it is reading and every place in the passband that looked like a signal, and it
  finds the widest stretch that is at least 150 Hz clear of all of them, and calls
  in the middle of it. On an empty band that is 1300 Hz. **You do not choose a
  frequency and you cannot land on top of somebody by accident.**
- It writes out the call in full - `CQ CQ CQ de KC3QIS KC3QIS KC3QIS pse K` - keys
  the radio, sends for about eleven and a half seconds, and unkeys.
- A card appears on the panel saying **Calling**, with the time. That is your
  receipt. It has no Log button and no station on it, because a call to everybody
  is not a contact with anybody.

**If the band is too crowded**, it does not call. It says so in a sentence - *the
band is too crowded here to call without landing on someone* - and tells you to
move the dial a little or wait. Nothing goes out.

**Press CQ again** and the receipt refreshes to the new time and the call goes out
again. You never get two receipts.

**When somebody comes back to you**, the receipt disappears and his card takes its
place. If two people come back, you get two cards and the receipt still goes - the
call was to everybody, so neither of them inherits it.

### Answering somebody else

Right-click a row that is somebody calling CQ and you get one item: **Answer
EI4GNB**. One click sends the answer **on his frequency**, and his card goes up
saying it is his turn.

From there his card shows you one button at a time: *Tell him how he is coming
through* after his answer, *Confirm, and sign off* after his report. One click,
one transmission. When both of you have said goodbye the card says **Finished** and
the Log appears on it.

**When Hamlet is not sure it is your turn, there is no button** and the card says
*"Waiting to be sure it is your turn before offering anything to send."* That is
deliberate: a button that appeared on a guess would be Hamlet transmitting in your
name on a reading it did not trust.

### The power offer, and touching nothing at the radio

Beside the transmit drive you will see a short paragraph:

> PSK31 sends a steady carrier, so it runs warmer than voice. Hamlet can set your
> radio's transmit power to 50% for you. Nothing else on the radio changes, and
> nothing is set unless you press this.

Two buttons: **Set my power to 50%** and **I will set it myself**. Press the first
and Hamlet sends one command to the radio and tells you it did. Press the second
and it sends nothing. Press neither and it sends nothing.

**There is nothing else to set.** The transmit drive above it is Hamlet's own
composing level, the same one FT8 already works at, and it has a sensible value
already. **Nothing asks you about the USB MOD Level, and nothing asks you to look
at a meter.**

### If the ALC sentence ever appears

It will read, roughly:

> Your radio is being driven harder than it wants to be: its own level control is
> working to hold the signal back, and that is what makes a PSK31 signal spread out
> and splatter over the people either side of you. Turn the transmit drive above
> down one step and send again.

That is the whole of it. **Turn the drive box down by one and send again**, and if
it goes away you are done. You do not need to know what ALC stands for, and Hamlet
will not turn it down for you, because a level you did not choose is a level you
cannot reason about later.

### What will look wrong but is not

- **The ALC sentence will never appear on a real radio yet.** Hamlet's rig poll has
  no command for reading the ALC meter - there is none anywhere in the code - so
  the reading is always absent and the sentence never fires. The record says
  `"measured":false`, which is the honest answer rather than a silence. Item 47.
- **The receipt has no Log button.** That is your ruling of 2026-09-11, kept.
- **The card has no countdown ring.** FT8 cards have one because there is a slot
  boundary to catch. PSK31 has none: the press is the moment.
- **Pressing CQ with no radio connected still composes the call and says so**, then
  says nothing went out because no radio is connected. That is this machine, and it
  is written down rather than left as silence.

### New, raised by this unit

**47. There is no CI-V read command for the ALC anywhere in the tree, so §R11's
sentence cannot fire on a real radio.**

*Ruling wanted:* where the read comes from.
*Reasoning:* §R11 says *during a PSK31 send Hamlet reads the ALC and, if it
deflects past the zone, says so in plain words.* `RigField.Alc` exists; there is no
`CivRead` for it, so `RigStateMonitor` never fills it and the reading is always
absent. The judgement, the sentence and the event are built and proved from a fed
reading; only the source is missing.
*What was rejected and why:* inventing the command byte. §0 and §4 forbid a
plausible command nobody can check against the manual, and `RigValue.Undocumented`
exists for exactly this case. The manual page that documents the ALC meter read
would close it in one line.

**48. The six events of a press are written in a different order from the one the
instruction lists, and the reason is a §0.0 one.**

*Ruling wanted:* none, unless the order matters to a reader downstream.
*Reasoning:* the instruction lists composed, stage, keyed, unkeyed, record,
radio-after-send. What is written is composed, stages, **record**, keyed, unkeyed,
alc, radio-after-send. `psk31_send_keyed` claims the port took the keying frame,
and only the finished run knows whether it did - a line written before the await
would claim a radio keyed on an evening when the port was dead. So the pair follows
the run, and the engine's own `ft8_transmission` lands between the stages and them.
*What was rejected and why:* bracketing the await, which would have matched the
listed order and lied on the failure case.

**49. `psk31_send_refused` has a new reason token, `no_transmit_path`.**

*Ruling wanted:* none; recorded so the set of tokens stays known.
*Reasoning:* the documented set was `bolt`, `cap`, `uncertain`, `mode`. `bolt` is
now dead - there is no bolt. `no_clear_spot` is the instruction's own. A press with
