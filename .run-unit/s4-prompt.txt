You are judging one thing and nothing else.

Below is section 4 of a work unit's report. Its heading is
"What's blocking us". The convention is that a unit writes here
any question that needs a ruling from the owner - and that an
empty section 4 is a real answer meaning nothing is blocked.

THE OWNER IS STOPPED FOR EXACTLY TWO THINGS - his ruling of
2026-09-12, narrowed by his ruling of 2026-09-23:

  1  anything that touches keying, transmit, or the radio's safety -
     in a project that is not a radio, what the project can make
     happen outside the machine it runs on
  2  a decision that changes what the product promises the operator -
     what a card asserts, what a click does, what is logged as true

MONEY IS NOT ONE OF THEM. Until 2026-09-23 the list had a third item,
money past the budget; the owner's plan now supplies the capacity, and
a question about spend, cost or budget is the unit's own to decide and
is NOT a ruling request.

WHAT THE SECOND MEANS - the owner's ruling of 2026-09-13, which defines it
and does not widen it: A promise is a fact the product states to the
operator about the radio, a contact, or a send - what was logged, what was
heard, what went out. THE WORDING OF A HINT, A LABEL, A TARGET, A CARD'S
NEXT LINE, OR A NAME IS NEVER A PROMISE AND NEVER A STOP.

A QUESTION ABOUT ANYTHING ELSE IS NOT A RULING REQUEST, however it is
worded - even when it offers options, and even when it says the work
is waiting on the owner. Layout, wording, a test's shape, a number, a
mechanism in the plan that arithmetic will not allow, a plan line a
later ruling contradicts: the unit's own recommendation is taken on
those, marked author's, overrulable, and the work goes on.

Many units write a sentence SAYING nothing is blocking rather
than leaving it blank. THAT IS NOT A RULING REQUEST. Neither is
a note, an observation, a thing reported for the record, or a
recommendation the unit has already acted on.

A RULING IS WANTED only where the text asks the owner to decide
something INSIDE ONE OF THE TWO, or says work is stopped until he
decides something inside one of the two. Where one question is
inside the two and others are outside, a ruling is wanted.

Answer with these lines and nothing else. Where a ruling is wanted, three:

VERDICT: ruling
WHICH: keying     or   WHICH: promise
WHY: one sentence, plain text, no punctuation beyond commas and full stops

WHICH names the one of the two it touches - keying for the first, promise
for the second. Where no ruling is wanted, two:
