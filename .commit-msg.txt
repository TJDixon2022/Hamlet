WHAT THE 456 ms IS AND IS NOT. It is the wall clock from the press to the last
sample the loopback was handed - the endpoint itself falling silent, which
includes the 200 ms already queued in the card's buffer and the capture's own
delivery lag. It is a DIFFERENT QUANTITY from unit 263's 15-20 ms, which is when
the sink's own call returned. No tighter bound than the tree's is asserted:
TheStopStopsARealEndpointTests.cs:187 asserts under a second and this reports
what it read.

THE SECOND RED, AND IT IS THE ONE THAT MATTERED. The window-height diagnosis in
the previous commit was WRONG - resized to 1400x1400 and the press still reached
nothing. The cause is that AN OPEN MenuFlyout IS A POPUP WITH A LIGHT-DISMISS
LAYER OVER THE WINDOW, and the next press lands on that layer instead of on the
control underneath it. The test now closes the menu after invoking the item,
which is what a click on a menu item does in the application, and the press
lands. This is a harness fidelity fault and NOT a product defect: in the running
application the flyout closes itself when an item is clicked.

The abort is proved, not edited. The wire at the end carries five frames - the
operator's abort and then the sequence's own on the cancelled path - and the
assertion is on the three that went out while it was running, which is
TheOperatorCanStopItTests' own precedent.

Criterion 2's decode and criterion 3's abort are in different transmissions on
purpose: a stopped transmission does not decode, and nothing in this method
