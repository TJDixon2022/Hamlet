================================================================================
THE THREE QUESTIONS TASK 3 ASKS, ANSWERED
================================================================================

1. DOES ANY GRID CLOSE THE GAP? YES, AND IT IS 4x2 - AND THE AXIS THAT CLOSES IT IS NOT
   THE ONE THE INSTRUCTION EXPECTED.

   Task 1d measured HEAD's deficit at -13 dB as 3 dB at the worst cell and 2 dB
   placement-averaged, against an on-grid ladder taken a decibel at a time. At 4x2 the
   worst cell goes from 19 of 36 to 34 of 36 and the placement average from 808 of 900 to
   894 of 900 - 89.8% to 99.3% - while the on-grid cell does not move at all, still 36 of
   36. Interpolated on the same on-grid ladder (100% at -14, 69.4% at -15, 22.2% at -16)
   HEAD's placement average of 89.8% sits at -14.3 dB and its worst cell's 52.8% at
   -15.4 dB; 4x2's 99.3% sits above -14 dB and its worst cell's 94.4% at about -14.2 dB.
   The gap closes from 2.4 dB at the worst cell to under half a decibel.

   At -15 dB, where HEAD keeps 194 of 900, 4x2 keeps 327 of 900 - not closed, but 69%
   more messages, and that rung is below where either placement decodes reliably.

2. DOES IT FIT IN THE SLOT? YES, WITH TWO ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE TO SPARE.

   151.3 ms against a 7.5 s slot is 2.0% of it, in a DEBUG build, on this machine. The
   budget judged against is 10% of the slot - 750 ms - which leaves the tab's own work,
   the SNR estimate, the waterfall paint and the ledger write nine tenths of the slot.
   HEAD spends 0.9%. Even 4x8, the densest grid tried, spends 3.0%.

3. DOES THE WRONG COUNT STAY AT ZERO? YES, AT EVERY GRID.

   0 wrong decodes in 2700 slots at each of the four grids, 10800 slots in all, and the
   count is reported per cell and per rung rather than summed at the end. A denser grid
   makes more hypotheses and more hypotheses is how a decoder starts inventing messages;
   it did not happen here, and it was looked for at every cell.

================================================================================
AND WHY THE FREQUENCY AXIS DESTROYS WHAT THE TIME AXIS REPAIRS
================================================================================

This is read out of the port rather than guessed at. Ft8Monitor.ProcessBlock analyses a
sliding frame of TransformLength = BlockSize x FrequencyOversampling samples, advancing it
by SubblockSize each sub-block. So FREQUENCY OVERSAMPLING LENGTHENS THE ANALYSIS WINDOW:
at 2 the transform spans two whole FT4 symbol periods, at 4 it spans four. FT4 is 4-FSK
and its tone changes every symbol, so a window spanning four symbols sees four different
tones and the Costas correlation the sync search is built on is smeared away. That is
exactly what the table shows - every grid with frequencyOversampling above 2 loses its
ON-GRID decodes first, which is not what an off-grid problem looks like.

TIME OVERSAMPLING COSTS NOTHING OF THE KIND. It changes only how often the same-length
frame is sampled, so it buys sub-symbol alignment resolution with no smearing at all - and
sub-symbol alignment is precisely the axis the off-grid loss lives on. At 4x2 every one of
the twenty-five cells at -13 dB is at 34 or better, including the frequency-offset ones,
which says the dominant off-grid loss was never in frequency.

The port is faithful in all of this. Nothing here is a defect in it: freq_osr is a
parameter of monitor_init and demo/decode_ft8.c passes 2, and 2 is the largest value that
does not span more than one FT4 symbol change. What the sweep found is that upstream's
number is right for the frequency axis and improvable on the time one.
