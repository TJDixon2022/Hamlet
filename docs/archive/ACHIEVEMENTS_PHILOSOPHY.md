# Why Hamlet has achievements

**Tim's, 2026-09-09. This document is the reason. Read it before adding, removing
or changing anything on the achievements screen, and before putting any mark on
the CQ list.**

**A session that writes achievement number 180 without this document will write the
wrong one.** The rules below are not preferences about a screen. They come from who
this is for.

---

## 1. Who this is for

**A licensed operator who has never made a contact.**

Not a beginner - he has held a licence for years. He has a radio, an antenna and
the privileges. What he has never had is a success. The bands were a mystery to be
earned and nobody handed him the way in.

**Then Hamlet reads FT8 and he can suddenly see the band.** That is the moment this
whole project exists for.

**And then it wears off.** Tim's words:

> *"They start, they see FT8. Ooh, I can read FT8. They go to FT4. Wow, I can do
> FT4. But unless you're in a contest, that can get boring quickly."*

**The achievements exist to catch him there.** Not to gamify a radio, and not to
score him. To keep giving him a next thing at the exact moment the last thing stops
being novel.

---

## 2. The one thing that would ruin it

**A wall of empty cards reads as failure to somebody who has felt like a failure at
this hobby for years.**

> *"I don't want to have 10,000 blank achievements. That's intimidating. And it
> just reeks of more failure."*

**This is the governing constraint and it outranks completeness.** A screen that
shows everything he has not done is a screen that tells him what he is bad at, and
he has had enough of those. Every rule below follows from it.

---

## 3. The rules

### 3.1 Nothing is visible until something adjacent has been earned

**A band's records do not exist until he has made a contact on that band.** A mode's
records do not exist until he has worked that mode. A region's card does not exist
until he has worked something in it.

**No empty 80 m tab. No ghost FT4 records. No Africa card on day one.**

The screen grows as he operates. **A new tab appearing is itself the reward.**

### 3.2 Every unlock reveals more than it fills

**This is the rule that makes it feel generous rather than grinding.**

His first Central America contact does not merely fill a card. It **reveals the
Central America group**, and inside it a handful he can now see are reachable -
Belize, Costa Rica, Panama, Guatemala. Four things to do that did not exist a minute
ago.

**One contact in, three or four possibilities out.**

### 3.3 A group reveals, but only its nearest few show

**Four new cards appearing is a gift. Forty is a chore list.**

When a group opens, show the handful nearest to reach. The rest of that group
unlocks as he goes.

### 3.4 There are two kinds and they do different jobs

**Records unlock on first contact.** Furthest on 40 m, weakest read on FT4, busiest
day. These are *look what you have collected*, and they would be discouraging as a
wall of blanks.

**Named challenges stay visible before they are earned.** First contact past 5,000
miles. First below -22. First on every continent. **These are *go and try this*, and
their whole purpose is to be a target**, so hiding them defeats them.

**A handful of challenges, always. Records only once opened.**

### 3.5 The achievement is the excuse; the learning is the point

**Write challenges that push toward things a new operator would not know to try.**

40 m at night is a different world from 20 m in the afternoon. Grey line is real
propagation. A contact below -22 dB is at the edge of what any decoder reads.

**Each challenge is a nudge toward something worth knowing**, and the `i` hover
carries the *why*: *grey line is the hour around sunrise and sunset when low bands
open up over long paths*. **That sentence is the actual product.** The card is the
reason he reads it.

### 3.6 It reaches the CQ list

**The achievements are not a page he visits. They change what he does.**

> *"What if the CQ list in FT8 highlighted countries that I haven't contacted yet?
> ...it becomes active on the CQ side of the digital modes."*

A station in an entity he has never worked is **marked in the list he is already
looking at**, so the nudge costs him no attention. Hovering says what it would earn.

**Nothing new is computed.** The list already resolves a callsign to an entity, and
the log already knows what he has worked. **The mark is the intersection.**

This inverts the worked-station fade already in the panel: **a station he has worked
dims; a station in a new country lifts.** Same axis, both ends used.

### 3.7 Restraint, or the mark means nothing

**On a busy evening half the list could be new countries.** If everything is marked,
nothing is.

- **Mark the strongest one or two per slot**, not every eligible row.
- **Rarity is proportionate.** A new US state is a small thing; a new continent is
  not. A mark that treats them alike cries wolf.
- **An unmarked station must not read as worthless.** The person he most wants to
  work might be an ordinary domestic contact. **That is still a QSO and the screen
  must not imply otherwise.**

### 3.8 Deep enough never to see the bottom

**150 to 250 achievements eventually, and eight or ten visible at a time.**

They come from what the log already holds and nothing is invented: **DXCC entities**
grouped into continents and sub-regions, **bands**, **modes**, **distance
thresholds**, **signal thresholds**, **time of day and grey line**, **grid squares**,
**US states**, **contacts per day and per hour**.

**Cross a few of those and 250 arrives without a single made-up category.**

---

## 4. What this does not license

- **No achievement claims an award he has not got.** DXCC and Worked All States are
  counted by **confirmations**, not contacts. **Hamlet counts *worked*, says
  *worked*, and never says *confirmed*** (§0.0).
- **No sub-region is presented as official.** DXCC defines continents. *Scandinavia*,
  *Central America* and *the Caribbean* are Hamlet's own groupings and the screen
  says so rather than implying a category somebody else recognises.
- **Nothing is scored, ranked against other operators, or shared anywhere.** This is
  his own record on his own machine.
- **No achievement transmits anything, and no mark on the CQ list arms a reply.**
  One click, one transmission (§0.2).
- **Nothing shames.** No streak he broke, no *you have not operated in 6 days*, no
  card that exists to tell him what he failed to do. **He has had enough of those.**

---

## 5. The test to apply to any new achievement

Before adding one, answer all four:

1. **Can it be computed from the log alone**, with no new data and no guess?
2. **Does it point at something worth trying** that he might not otherwise?
3. **Would seeing it unearned feel like an invitation or like a failure?** If the
   second, it unlocks rather than showing.
4. **Does its `i` hover teach him something true about radio?**

**If any answer is no, it does not belong on the screen.**
