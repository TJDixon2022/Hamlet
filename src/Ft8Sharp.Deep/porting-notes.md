# Porting notes — Ft8Sharp.Deep

**This library is not a port.** `Ft8Sharp` is the port — a faithful MIT rendering of
`ft8_lib`, byte-identical in behaviour, which this phase changes not one line of.
`Ft8Sharp.Deep` is where everything that tries to *hear better than* the port lives,
and it is GPL-3.0.

This file exists for the same reason the port's does: so that nobody has to
reconstruct later, from commit messages, where an algorithm came from and what it
deliberately is not.

**It is a separate file from `src/Ft8Sharp/porting-notes.md` on purpose.**
`PHASE_PLAN.md` step 2 asks for the ordered statistics citation *in
`porting-notes.md`*, and the only `porting-notes.md` in this tree when unit 246 was
authored sat inside the project this phase may not touch. The citation is here, and
it is in XML remarks at the point of use as well.

---

## Provenance of the algorithms

**Nothing in this library came from WSJT-X's source or from `ft4_ft8_public/`.**
Neither was read. `ft8_lib`, which `Ft8Sharp` is a port of, has no ordered
statistics decoder at all, so there was nothing to port even if porting had been the
intent.

Every algorithm here is implemented from a published description:

| What | Source |
|---|---|
| Ordered statistics decoding — reliability ordering, the most reliable basis, order-λ reprocessing, ranking by soft distance | M. P. C. Fossorier and S. Lin, *Soft-decision decoding of linear block codes based on ordered statistics*, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory **41**(5), September 1995, pages 1379–1396 |
| Soft combining of repeated transmissions — adding the log-likelihood ratios of two conditionally independent observations of the same codeword bit | **Standard soft-decision theory, and nobody's implementation.** If `L1` and `L2` are the log-odds a bit is one given each observation, the log-odds given both is `L1 + L2`, because the observations are conditionally independent given the bit and the log of a product is a sum. Any coding text carries it; `ft8_lib` has nothing of the kind to port and neither WSJT-X's source nor `ft4_ft8_public/` was read |
| The protocol these codewords carry — the (174, 91) LDPC code, the 77-bit payload, the CRC-14 | S. Franke K9AN, B. Somerville G4WJS and J. Taylor K1JT, *The FT4 and FT8 Communication Protocols*, QEX, July/August 2020 |
| Baseband downconversion of one candidate — multiplication by a complex exponential, a windowed-sinc low-pass, decimation | **Standard multirate digital signal processing, and nobody's implementation.** Oppenheim and Schafer, *Discrete-Time Signal Processing*; Lyons, *Understanding Digital Signal Processing*; Harris, *Multirate Signal Processing for Communication Systems*. The whole arithmetic behind the three numbers this library uses — decimation 24, 401 taps, a 150 Hz cutoff — is written out in `Ft8DeepBasebandSettings`, and it is derived from the tone span and the alias bands rather than tuned |
| Fine synchronisation by Costas correlation — correlating the known sync sequence against the received signal at a grid of sub-symbol time and sub-hertz frequency offsets and taking the maximum | **Standard detection theory, and nobody's implementation.** The statistic is the non-coherent matched-filter gain `\|sum x e^(-j2 pi f n)\|^2 / (L sum \|x\|^2)`, which is one for a pure tone and about `1/L` for white noise. Any communications text carries it |
| The frame the two rows above are applied to — 79 symbols of 0.160 s, eight tones spaced 6.25 Hz, three seven-symbol Costas arrays at symbols 0, 36 and 72 | The same QEX paper. The arrays themselves are the port's `Ft8Tables.Ft8CostasPattern` and the layout is the port's `Ft8SymbolEncoder` |

**The QEX paper is what makes the combining arithmetic mean anything.** Adding two
vectors position by position is only a combination of *evidence about one
transmission* because position *i* is the same codeword bit in every hearing, and
because the thing the sum is finally judged by is the CRC-14 the payload carries.
Both are the protocol's facts, from that paper, and they are cited in XML remarks on
`Ft8DeepSoftCombiner` at the point of use as well as here.

**WSJT-X is a measuring instrument in this phase and never a source.** It is what a
capture fixture's expected list is generated by; it is not where an algorithm comes
from. **This binds hardest on the baseband re-sync**, because WSJT-X downconverts and
re-syncs candidates too. Its source was not read, no downconverter, resampler or
fine-sync implementation was looked for, and the design above was derived from the
tone span, the alias bands and the sinc loss — each of which is written out beside
the number it produced.

`NOTICE` carries both citations, and carried them before a line of either was
written — that was step 1's fourth exit, closed by unit 245.

## The generator, and where it comes from

`Ft8DeepOrderedStatistics` needs the 91 × 174 generator **G** of the code. It does
not unpack it from `Ft8Tables.LdpcGenerator`, whose 83 rows are the *parity checks*
in upstream's own packing. It reads it off the port's own encoder instead:

> The code is systematic in its first 91 bits — `Ft8CodewordDecoder` packs
> `codewordBits[..91]` straight as the payload — so row *i* of **G** is exactly what
> `LdpcEncoder.Encode` returns for the payload with bit *i* set and every other bit
> clear.

**And it is checked rather than assumed.**
`Ft8Unit246CeilingTests.TheGeneratorReadOffTheEncoderReproducesEveryEncodeItIsCheckedAgainst`
asserts that the 91 rows form the identity on columns 0–90, and that 500 random
payloads encode bit for bit the same through the derived **G** by GF(2) arithmetic
and through `LdpcEncoder.Encode`. Every bit agreed. A mistake here would poison every
number this library produces and would look exactly like an algorithm that does not
work.

## What this library does not do, and must not

**It does not decide that a message is real.** There is no checksum in this library,
no threshold, no confidence and no acceptance rule. A codeword that
`Ft8DeepOrderedStatistics` produces is submitted to the port's
`Ft8CodewordDecoder.Decode` as saturated ratios, and the port's own **parity gate**
and **CRC-14 gate** are the only acceptance. That is `CLAUDE.md` §0.0 at the exact
place a tired session would fudge it.

**One codeword per candidate is submitted, and no more.** Every codeword put to the
CRC-14 is an independent chance of a false accept at about one in 16 384. A slot's
140 candidates times a search of thousands of re-encodings, all submitted, would put
tens of messages nobody sent in front of the operator — and it would fail quietly,
because each one would carry a valid checksum. Only the single best-ranked codeword
per candidate goes to the gate.

**The de-duplication key for an OSD decode is the codeword OSD already has**, its
first 77 bits — not a re-run of `LdpcDecoder.Decode` over the original ratios, which
is what the port does and which would fail for exactly the candidates OSD rescued.

## Divergences from the port, deliberately

1. **The per-candidate loop is reproduced, not delegated to.** `Ft8DeepSlotDecoder`
   runs `Ft8SlotDecoder.Decode(Ft8Waterfall)`'s stages itself, through the port's
   public members, because ordered statistics decoding has to sit *inside* that loop
   at the point where a candidate fails parity and there is nowhere else to put it.
   This is route A of `docs/unit245-deep-seam.md` §4. It needs no
   `InternalsVisibleTo` and no change to the port.

2. **With OSD off the reproduction is exact, and that is enforced.**
   `Ft8DeepIdentityTests` compares the whole `Ft8SlotResult` — all five counts and
   every message's text, candidate, frequency and dt, in order — against
   `Ft8SlotDecoder` over one 51-trial ladder block at -19 dB, one at -21 dB, the
   committed capture, and all 69 reference recordings. Without that test a difference
   between the scoreboard's OSD-off and OSD-on columns would no longer be
   attributable to OSD.

3. **The port's search is not exposed by the port**, only its candidate limit and its
   minimum score. `Ft8DeepSlotDecoder(Ft8SlotDecoder)` therefore rebuilds a search
   from those two, which carries this library's default block-offset sweep. A caller
   who gave the port a search with a non-default sweep must pass the same search
   here. Said out loud rather than left to be discovered.

## Numbers this library was built against

Measured by unit 246, task 1, over one whole 51-message ladder block at **-21 dB**
with the harness's own seed, frequency and offset. For every candidate the sync
search returned, the hard decision was compared against the codeword the ladder
*knows* it transmitted.

- The closest candidate's total hard-decision distance ran **15 to 45 in 49 of 51
  trials**, median about 31 — which is unit 222's figure, against a code whose
  iterative recovery reaches zero at about 17.
- Of those errors, the number falling inside the **91 most reliable positions** ran
  **0 to 12 in 49 of 51 trials**, median **6**.
- Two trials had no candidate within 60 of the truth at all, which is a
  synchronisation finding rather than a coding one.

**That distribution is the ceiling on this approach**, because an order-λ decode can
only reach the transmitted codeword when the basis carries at most λ errors. It is
written up in `docs/unit246-osd.md`.
